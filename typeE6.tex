\section{The p-adic group of type \texorpdfstring{$E_6$}{PDFstring}}\label{sec:E6}

In this section, we let $G=E_6(F)_{\scn}$ be the simple $p$-adic of type $E_6$ of simply connected type. Its affine Dynkin diagram is \begin{center}
    \begin{tikzpicture}[scale=1, baseline=-3pt, circ/.style={draw, circle, inner sep=2pt, minimum size=2.5mm}]
        \node[circ, label=above:$\alpha_1$] (1) at (0,0) {}; 
        \node[circ, label=above:$\alpha_3$] (2) at (1.5,0) {}; 
        \node[circ, label=above:$\alpha_4$] (3) at (3,0) {}; 
        \node[circ, label=above:$\alpha_5$] (4) at (4.5,0) {};
        \node[circ, label=above:$\alpha_6$] (5) at (6,0) {};
        \node[circ, label=right:$\alpha_2$] (6) at (3,-1.5) {}; 
        \node[circ, label=right:$-\alpha_0$] (7) at (3,-3) {}; 
        \draw (1)--(2) (2)--(3) (3)--(4) (4)--(5) (3)--(6) (6)--(7);
    \end{tikzpicture}
\end{center}
and its fundamental group is $\Omega\cong\ZZ/3\ZZ$. The group $G$ has $7$ conjugacy classes of open maximal open compact subgroups $K_i,\ i=0,1,\dots,6$, one for each $J_i=S_{\aff}\setminus\{\calpha_i\},\ i=0,1,\ldots,6$. In this section, we prove
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:E6sc}
    Let $G$ be a simple p-adic group of type $E_6$ of simply connected type. Then the diagram 
    \[
        \begin{tikzcd}
            R_{\un,\mathrm{ell}}(G) \arrow[r, "\FT^\vee_{\mathrm{ell}}"] \arrow[d, "\res_{\un}^{\mathrm{cpt}}"] & R_{\un,\mathrm{ell}}(G) \arrow[d, "\res_{\un}^{\mathrm{cpt}}"] \\
            \bigoplus_{i=0}^6 R_{\un}(\overline{K}_i) \arrow[r, "(\FT^{K_i})_i"] & \bigoplus_{i=0}^6 R_{\un}(\overline{K}_i),
        \end{tikzcd}
    \]
    commutes, up to roots of unity.
\end{theorem}


The classes parametrized by $J_0,J_1,J_6$ and $J_2,J_3,J_5$ are conjugate under the action of $\Omega$, respectively, and in particular they have the same reductive type. By Proposition \ref{prop:omega_action}, conditional to the verification of condition \eqref{eqn:condition}, it is enough to prove the conjecture for the conjugacy classes of maximal open compact subgroups corresponding to $J_0$, $J_2$ and $J_4$. 

Next, we classify the elliptic pairs in $G^\vee=E_6(\CC)_{\ad}$. This classification is well known in the literature for $E_6(\CC)_{\scn}$, but for $E_6(\CC)_{\ad}$ is slightly more involved. For most unipotent elements $u\in G^\vee$, the reductive part $\Gamma_u$ of its centralizer is connected. When this is the case, there exists a precise description of the elliptic pairs in $\Gamma_u$.

\begin{proposition}\cite{AubertCiubotaruRomano2024}*{Proposition 8.7}
    Suppose that $\Gamma_u$ is connected. Then there is a bijection
    $$\Gamma_u\backslash\mathcal{Y}(\Gamma_u)_{\textrm{ell}}\leftrightarrow W(\Gamma_u)\backslash\{(s,w)\ |\ s\in T\text{ is regular, } w\in W(\Gamma_u)\text{ is elliptic, }s\in T^w\},$$
    where the map is obtained by conjugating an elliptic pair $(s,h)$ so that $s$ lies in a fixed torus $T$ of $\Gamma_u$ and $h$ is the lift of some $w\in W(\Gamma_u)$ such that $s\in T^w$.
\end{proposition}

With this in mind we can give a precise description of the elliptic pairs inside $G^\vee=E_6(\CC)_{\ad}$. We use the same notation as in Table \ref{tbl:F4_pairs}.

Let us briefly describe the elliptic pairs above explicitly. When $\Gamma_u^0=\{1\}$ the description is straightforward. Next, consider the semisimple matrices 
$$s_3=\begin{pmatrix}
    1 & 0 & 0 \\
    0 & \zeta_3 & 0 \\
    0 & 0 & \zeta_3^2 
\end{pmatrix}\quad\text{and}\quad w_3=\begin{pmatrix}
    0 & 1 & 0\\
    0 & 0 & 1\\
    1 & 0 & 0
\end{pmatrix}\quad\text{in }\SL_3(\CC).$$

We also let $s_{3,3}=(s_3,s_3)$ and $w_{3,3}=(w_3,w_3)$ be matrices in $\SL_3\times\SL_3$. We abuse notation and denote their images in central quotients with the same symbols as necessary.

When $u$ has type $D_4$, then $\Gamma_u\backslash\mathcal{Y}\mathcal(\Gamma_u)_{\text{ell}}=\{(s_3,w_3),(s_3,w_3^{-1})\}$ and $A_{us_3}=R(u)\cong C_3$, while if $u$ has type $A_2$, then $\Gamma_u\backslash\mathcal{Y}\mathcal(\Gamma_u)_{\text{ell}}=\{(s_{3,3},w_{3,3}),(s_{3,3},w_{3,3}^{-1})\}$ and $A_{us_{3,3}}=A_{us_{3,3}}^0\times R(u)\cong C_2\times C_3$. The remaining case when $u$ has type $D_4(a_1)$ is the most interesting. Then $\Gamma_u=(\CC^\times)^2\rtimes S_3$, where the $3$-cycle $(123)$ in $S_3$ acts by $(a,b)\mapsto(b,\frac{1}{ab})$ and the $2$-cycle $(12)$ acts by $(a,b)\mapsto(\frac{1}{b},\frac{1}{a})$. A lengthy computation then shows that $\Gamma_u$ has $12$ elliptic pairs up to conjugacy. To describe them, let 
$$1=((1,1),e),\ z=((\zeta_3,\zeta_3),e),\ g_3=((1,1),(123))\in\Gamma_u.$$
Then the $12$ elliptic pairs up to conjugacy are
$\{(z^k,g_3),(g_3,z^k),(g_3,z^kg_3),(g_3,z^kg_3^{-1})\ |\ k=0,1,2\},$
where
$$A_u=A_u^0=S_3,\quad A_{uz}=R(u)=\langle g_3\rangle\cong C_3\quad\text{and}\quad A_{ug_3}=A_{ug_3}^0\times R(u)=\langle g_3\rangle\times\langle z\rangle\cong C_3\times C_3$$
Thus, $R_{\un,\text{ell}}(G)$ is a $22$-dimensional vector space, and the proof of 
\ref{thm:E6sc} relies again on an explicit computation of $\FT^\vee_{\mathrm{ell}}$ and the parahoric restriction maps
$$\res_{\un}^{K_i}:R_{\un,\text{ell}}(G)\longrightarrow R_{\un}(\overline{K}_i),\ \text{ for } i=0,2,4.$$
Next, we discuss the action of $\FT^\vee_{\mathrm{ell}}$ on the basis elements $\Pi(u,s,h)$. To this aim, let $C:G^\vee\to G^\vee$ be a Chevalley involution and let $T^\vee\subset G^\vee$ a maximal torus such that $C(t)=t^{-1}$ for all $t\in T^\vee$. When $u$ has type $E_6$, $E_6(a_1)$ and $E_6(a_3)$, all automorphisms of $\Gamma_u$ are inner, and therefore 
$$\FT^\vee_{\mathrm{ell}}(\Pi(u,s,h))=\Pi(u,h,s).$$ 
When $u$ has type $D_4$ or $D_4(a_1)$, we fix $L^\vee\subset G^\vee$ to be a standard Levi subgroup of type $D_4$ containing $T^\vee$. Since maximal tori in $\Gamma_u$ have rank two, the centre $Z(L^\vee)$ is a maximal torus of $\Gamma_u$ contained in $T^\vee$. In addition, the Chevalley involution acts by $C(\mathcal{X}_\alpha)=\mathcal{X}_{-\alpha}$ on root spaces, so it preserves $L^\vee$. Inside $L^\vee$, the element $u$ is either regular or subregular, so there exists some $l\in L^\vee$ such that $lC(u)l^{-1}=u$. Therefore, the map $\inn(l)\circ C:\Gamma_u\to\Gamma_u$ is a Chevalley involution since $\inn(l)(C(t))=t^{-1}$ for all $t\in Z(L^\vee)\subset T^\vee$. When $u$ has type $A_2$, an analogous argument shows that there is some $l$ in a standard Levi $L^\vee$ of type $A_2$ containing $T^\vee$ such that $\inn(l)\circ C$ is a Chevalley involution of $\Gamma_u$. In section \ref{subsec:SLn}, we showed that 
\begin{align*}
    \FT^\vee_{\mathrm{ell}}:\ &\Pi(D_4,s_3,w_3^{\pm1})\longmapsto\Pi(D_4,s_3,w_3^{\pm1}),\\
    & \Pi(A_2,s_{3,3},w_{3,3}^{\pm1})\longmapsto\Pi(A_2,s_{3,3},w_{3,3}^{\pm1}).
\end{align*}
Finally, when $u$ has type $D_4(a_1)$, $\Gamma_u=(\CC^\times)^2\rtimes S_3$ and the automorphism $C_u(((a,b),\tau))=((\frac{1}{a},\frac{1}{b}),\tau)$ is a Chevalley involution. Thus, we have that $\FT^\vee_{\mathrm{ell}}$ swaps the basis vectors
\begin{align*}
    \Pi(u,1,g_3)\longleftrightarrow\Pi(u,g_3,1),\quad \Pi(u,z,g_3)&\longleftrightarrow\Pi(u,g_3,z^2),\quad\Pi(u,z^2,g_3)\longleftrightarrow\Pi(u,g_3,z),\\
    \Pi(u,g_3,z^kg_3^{\pm1})&\longleftrightarrow\Pi(u,g_3,z^kg_3^{\pm1}),\quad k=0,1,2.
    %\Pi(u,g_3,g_3^{\pm1})\longleftrightarrow\Pi(u,g_3,g_3^{\pm1}),\quad \Pi(u,g_3,zg_3^{\pm1})\longleftrightarrow\Pi(u,g_3,zg_3^{\pm1}),\quad\Pi(u,g_3,z^2g_3^{\pm1})\longleftrightarrow\Pi(u,g_3,z^2g_3^{\pm1}).
\end{align*}
\begin{lemma}
    Condition \eqref{eqn:condition} holds for all $\omega\in\Omega$, $u\in G^\vee$ unipotent and $(s,h)\in\mathcal{Y}(\Gamma_u)_{\mathrm{ell}}$. In particular, if Diagram \eqref{eqn:diagram} commutes for parahoric subgroups $K_0,K_2$ and $K_4$, then Theorem \ref{thm:E6sc} holds.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
    If the ordered triple $(C(u),C(h),C(s))$ is conjugate to the ordered triple $(u,s,h)$, then condition \eqref{eqn:condition} holds trivially. On the other hand, by Remark \ref{rem:condition}, condition \eqref{eqn:condition} holds when either $s=1$ or $h=1$. By the classification of elliptic pairs above, it only remains to prove condition \eqref{eqn:condition} when $u$ has type $D_4(a_1)$ and $(s,h)\in\{(z,g_3),(z^2,g_3)\}$. \textcolor{red}{One can directly prove that, up to a choice of primitive third root of unity, $$\overline{\tau}_{uz}(\omega)(g_3)=\theta,\quad\text{and}\quad\overline{\tau}_{ug_3}(\omega)(z)=\theta^2,$$ from which the Lemma easily follows.}
\end{proof}

Finally, let us describe the unipotent blocks and the Langlands parameters for $G$. There are $8$ unipotent blocks, $6$ of which are supercuspidal, one for each pair $(K_0,\sigma)$, where $K_0\cong E_6(\mathcal{O}_F)$ is a hyperspecial parahoric subgroup (there are three conjugacy classes in $G$) and $\sigma\in\{E_6[\theta],E_6[\theta^2]\}$ is a cuspidal unipotent representation of $E_6(\FF_q)$. The Hecke algebras $\cH(E_6(F),K_0,\sigma)\cong\CC$ so the block contains a unique Langlands parameter, which can be read from Lusztig's arithmetic-geometric tables (when $G$ is adjoint), combined with Solleveld's results (\cite{Solleveld2020}*{Lemma 7.3}).

The principal block contains all representations $\pi(us,\phi)$ such that $H(\mathcal{B}^u_s)^\phi\neq 0$ and the corresponding Hecke algebra $\cH(E_6(F),\mathcal{I},\mathbf{1})$ is the affine Hecke algebra of type $E_6$ of equal parameters. The parahoric restrictions are computed using the methods described in Section \ref{sec:parahoric_restriction}.

Finally, there is one intermediate Hecke algebra $\cH(E_6(F),D_4(\mathcal{O}_F),\sigma)$, where $\sigma$ is the unique cuspidal unipotent representation of $D_4(\FF_q)$. The only Langlands parameter we encounter corresponding to this Hecke algebra is $[A_1A_5,-]$, where $u$ has type $E_6(a_3)$, $s$ is the unique non-trivial semisimple element commuting with $u$ (of order $2$), and $\phi$ is non-trivial.
