\section{Parahoric restriction tables}\label{sec:tables}

We now display the parahoric restriction tables obtained by applying the methods described in Section \ref{sec:parahoric_restriction} together with the geometric data from $F_4(\CC)$ and $E_6(\CC)_{\ad}$ discussed in Sections \ref{sec:F4} and \ref{sec:E6}. For each maximal parahoric subgroup $K$ of $F_4(F)$ or $E_6(F)_{\scn}$, we display two tables. The first table contains the parahoric restrictions of the relevant unipotent representations arising in the virtual representations $\Pi(u,s,h)$. The second table then contains the parahoric restrictions of $\Pi(u,s,h)$ and is easily obtained from the first by computing the corresponding linear combinations. The first columns the second tables are the characters in trivial families; then we write each family with the special character always coming first. We omit the tables for the maximal hyperspecial subgroup $K_0$ of $F_4(F)$ for the tables are lengthy and the conjecture was already verified by Ciubotaru in \cite{Ciubotaru2020}. From these tables, together with Lusztig's nonabelian Fourier transform, Theorems \ref{thm:F4} and \ref{thm:E6sc} can be easily verified

For all tables, we use the notation described in \ref{notation:Langlandsparam} for the Langlands labels parametrizing the unipotent representations of $G=F_4(F)$, coinciding with Reeder's notation in \cite{Reeder2000}. The unipotent representations of finite reductive groups of Lie type are labelled as follows. For groups of type $A_n$, they are parametrized by partitions $\lambda$ of $n+1$. For groups of type $B_n/C_n$, the principal series representations are parametrized by double partitions $[\lambda,\mu]$ of $n$. To understand these tables, it is enough to know that groups of type $B_3/C_3$ have two non-principal series representations $\theta_1$ and $\theta_2$, while groups of type $B_4/C_4$ have five non-principal series representations $\theta_1,\ldots,\theta_5$. They all lie in the same representation series, arising from the unipotent cuspidal representation of their Levi subgroup of type $B_2/C_2$ and of all them are part of a family of size $4$ of unipotent representations.

Finally, we use the following useful notation shorthand. A finite group of Lie type of type $A_1$ has only two unipotent characters; the trivial and the Steinberg. Thus, whenever the type of reductive quotient is the product of two simple types, one of which is $A_1$, we give the multiplicities for each character $\chi$ of the other type as a pair $a,\overline{b}$, where $a$ and $\overline{b}$ are the multiplicities of $\chi\otimes\mathbf{1}_{A_1}$ and $\chi\otimes\St_{A_1}$, respectively.
